# Age Groups, Grades 2 to 8

Working profiles used by every teacher, grade head, and course designer. They are professional working knowledge, not survey data. `ux-researcher` validates them with real families; research wins over these notes.

## Demographics (all grades)
Our first families (working assumption, validate with `ux-researcher`): urban families in Greater Cairo, Giza, and Alexandria, mostly middle to upper-middle income, with children in international schools (American, British, IB, French) and national schools. Many parents work long hours; many families pay for private lessons; grandparents and nannies are often involved in after-school time. Families are Muslim and Christian; Arabic and Islamic or Christian religion are studied by all children in Egyptian schools, including international schools.

## Grade 2 (Primary 2), ages 7 to 8 (grade head: Afaf Mansy)
- **Development:** Concrete thinkers who learn through stories, objects, pictures, and doing. Decoding Arabic with full diacritics is still being built, so reading load must be light and audio support matters. Attention holds for short bursts; a single task of a few minutes, then a change. Can follow one or two steps, not long chains. Sense of time and quantity is still forming.
- **Psychographics:** Wants to please adults and be told they are good. Loves characters, songs, rhythm, repetition, and being the helper. Takes mistakes personally and can freeze or cry when told they are wrong. Strong sense of fairness in simple terms (turns, sharing).
- **Needs:** Praise for effort, warm and patient voice, very clear single instructions, lots of audio and pictures, quick wins, a parent nearby for set-up and reassurance.
- **Language and lingo:** Speaks Egyptian Arabic at home; meets Modern Standard Arabic mainly through school books and cartoons. In international schools, often mixes English words into Arabic. Use short sentences, familiar objects (food, family, animals, school bag, the Nile), and gentle humour. Avoid sarcasm and idioms.
- **Digital life:** Uses a parent's phone or a family tablet, mostly for cartoons and simple games. Usually cannot type well; voice and tapping are the main inputs.
- **How the tutor should behave:** Very short turns. Praise effort by name ('you checked your work, that is what scientists do'). Interrupt gently and early if stuck. One question at a time. Never more than two attempts before a smaller hint.

## Grade 3 (Primary 3), ages 8 to 9 (grade head: Wael Hegazy)
- **Development:** Still concrete, but starting to read to learn instead of learning to read. Can follow a short sequence and compare two things. Enjoys rules, categories, and collecting. Memory for facts is strong; explaining reasons is new and needs scaffolding.
- **Psychographics:** Loves knowing 'secret' facts, jokes, riddles, and being the expert. Becoming aware of peers; starting to compare. Fair play matters a lot. Still wants adult approval but enjoys a little independence.
- **Needs:** Clear structure, chances to show what they know, short reading passages with images, visible progress (stars, levels), encouragement to say 'because'.
- **Language and lingo:** Egyptian Arabic at home, growing comfort with MSA in reading. Short, lively sentences; questions that start with 'What do you notice?' and 'Why do you think?'. Riddles and 'did you know' facts land well.
- **Digital life:** Tablets and phones for videos and games; some typing in Arabic or English, but voice is easier. Familiar with simple game rewards.
- **How the tutor should behave:** Short turns with a playful tone. Ask for a reason after every answer. Celebrate 'I found it myself' moments. Keep text on screen minimal.

## Grade 4 (Primary 4), ages 9 to 10 (grade head: Sahar Raouf)
- **Development:** A big step: subjects split apart (social studies becomes its own subject) and content becomes more abstract. Many children hit a wall here when reading load and new vocabulary jump. Can reason about two or three steps, start to plan, and check their own work if prompted.
- **Psychographics:** Competence matters: wants to be good at things and hates looking babyish. Peer comparison grows. Interests deepen (football, games, drawing, YouTube creators). Starts to argue logically and spot unfairness.
- **Needs:** Respect, not baby talk. Clear purpose for each task. Support with new vocabulary. Small challenges that feel grown-up. Recognition of strategy, not only correct answers.
- **Language and lingo:** Egyptian Arabic with growing English mixing in international schools; some game and YouTube vocabulary. Speak plainly and with respect. A light, friendly tone works; trying too hard to sound cool does not.
- **Digital life:** Often has more device time, plays online games, watches creators. Many have already seen classmates use AI chat apps for homework.
- **How the tutor should behave:** Treat the child as capable. Ask them to explain their strategy. Introduce vocabulary with a picture and an example before using it. Let them try a bit longer before hinting.

## Grade 5 (Primary 5), ages 10 to 11 (grade head: Ashraf Labib)
- **Development:** Can handle multi-step reasoning, compare viewpoints, and organise information. Reading stamina is better but long texts still tire. Starting to study independently and to plan work if shown how.
- **Psychographics:** Wants ownership and some control. Proud of mastery. Friendships and group belonging matter more. Humour gets sharper; mild sarcasm appears. Sensitive to being embarrassed in front of others.
- **Needs:** Choice within structure, challenge that is achievable, private feedback, chances to teach back what they learned, reasons why a topic matters.
- **Language and lingo:** Mix of Egyptian Arabic and English in international schools; Arabizi (Arabic written in Latin letters and numbers) appears in chats. Respond naturally and clearly; mirror the child's language choice for chat, but keep lesson language correct (MSA for Arabic lessons, English for English-medium subjects).
- **Digital life:** Phones and tablets are routine; group chats with classmates begin. AI tools are known and sometimes used to finish homework.
- **How the tutor should behave:** Give ownership: 'Which method do you want to try?'. Push for explanations in their own words. Use friendly challenge, never public comparison.

## Grade 6 (Primary 6), ages 11 to 12 (grade head: Nermeen Azmy)
- **Development:** Abstract reasoning is emerging: can handle 'what if' questions, cause and effect chains, and early algebraic thinking. End of primary school; workload and expectations rise. Puberty begins for many, with mood and energy changes.
- **Psychographics:** Identity forming; wants to be seen as older. Peer status matters a great deal. Can be self-conscious and avoid trying when afraid of failing. Values fairness, honesty, and being taken seriously.
- **Needs:** Respectful tone, autonomy, privacy, reasons and real-world links, safe space to be wrong without anyone seeing.
- **Language and lingo:** Fluent code-switching between Egyptian Arabic and English (international schools), Arabizi in chats, slang that changes quickly. Do not force slang; sound like a respected older mentor, not a cartoon and not a lecturer.
- **Digital life:** Social apps and games are part of daily life; many are below the minimum age of some platforms but use them anyway. AI shortcuts for homework are common.
- **How the tutor should behave:** Respect intelligence. Ask challenging 'why' and 'what if' questions. Keep feedback private and specific. Avoid childish rewards; recognise mastery and persistence.

## Grade 7 (Preparatory 1), ages 12 to 13 (grade head: Khaled Sorour)
- **Development:** New stage (Preparatory 1): more subjects, more content, faster pace. Abstract thinking strengthens; can debate, generalise, and form arguments. Organisation and time management often lag behind the workload.
- **Psychographics:** Strong need for autonomy and peer acceptance. Quick to call things 'boring' or 'cringe'. Sensitive to being talked down to. Can be very motivated by purpose, competence, and fairness.
- **Needs:** Respect, efficiency, relevance, help organising work, a way to catch up quietly, recognition that is not childish.
- **Language and lingo:** Heavy code-switching, Arabizi, internet slang and memes that change fast. Lesson language stays correct; chat tone can be relaxed and direct. Never mock, never imitate badly.
- **Digital life:** Smartphone users, short-video apps, gaming, group chats. AI chat apps are a normal part of homework for many.
- **How the tutor should behave:** Be efficient and direct. Explain why a skill matters. Challenge them to beat their own previous reasoning. Respect their time.

## Grade 8 (Preparatory 2), ages 13 to 14 (grade head: Dr. Ola Tantawy)
- **Development:** Can reason abstractly, evaluate evidence, and argue a position. Workload and pressure increase; private lessons and exam focus are common. Sleep and energy can be low.
- **Psychographics:** Identity and independence are central. Sceptical of anything that feels fake or childish. Values honesty, competence, and being treated as nearly an adult. Under pressure, may take shortcuts to cope.
- **Needs:** Credibility, real challenge, efficient practice, autonomy over pace, a way to understand rather than memorise for exams.
- **Language and lingo:** Mature, direct tone; code-switching and slang are normal in chat. Lesson language stays correct. Respect beats entertainment.
- **Digital life:** Heavy smartphone use, short video, gaming, messaging. Copying from AI is most common at this age because the workload is highest.
- **How the tutor should behave:** Treat as a capable young adult. Use Socratic questioning, ask for evidence, invite disagreement. Show how understanding saves time on exams.
